# African Languages and Cultural Preservation Vision

## 1. Vision

DukeAI Nexus aims to contribute to the preservation, accessibility, and responsible development of African languages and cultural knowledge through open-source technology and community-centred research.

The long-term vision is to support locally accessible tools that help people learn, document, explore, and share knowledge across African languages and communities.

**Our Languages. Our Heritage. Our Future.**

## 2. Why This Matters

Africa has an extraordinary diversity of languages, oral traditions, knowledge systems, artistic practices, and histories.

Many languages and cultural resources face challenges involving:

- Limited digital representation.
- Inadequate language technology.
- Fragmented documentation.
- Unequal access to digital learning resources.
- Limited availability of locally developed tools.
- Loss of intergenerational knowledge transmission.
- Inadequate funding for language and cultural research.

Technology can help address some of these challenges, but it must be developed responsibly and with meaningful community participation.

## 3. Initial Research Focus

The first technical focus is English and Kiswahili civic and scientific learning.

The current prototype supports local document ingestion and lexical retrieval using demonstration material in these languages.

The wider African language vision is a long-term research direction, not a claim that the current prototype supports all African languages.

## 4. Long-Term Language Goals

Potential research directions include:

- African language documentation.
- Multilingual knowledge access.
- Language-specific text processing.
- Translation research.
- Language learning resources.
- Dialect and regional variation documentation.
- Community-reviewed dictionaries and glossaries.
- Digital accessibility for underrepresented languages.
- Locally executable language technology.

Future language work should be developed with speakers, linguists, educators, translators, and relevant communities.

## 5. Cultural Heritage and Creative Expression

The project recognises that cultural heritage includes more than written language.

Potential areas of future research and documentation include:

### 5.1 Oral Traditions

- Oral histories.
- Storytelling traditions.
- Proverbs and expressions.
- Community narratives.
- Intergenerational knowledge transmission.

### 5.2 Music and Performing Arts

- Traditional and contemporary music.
- Musical instruments.
- Dance and performance traditions.
- Music education and documentation.

### 5.3 Visual Arts and Craft

- Weaving and textiles.
- Sculpture and carving.
- Pottery and ceramics.
- Beadwork.
- Painting and illustration.
- Architecture and design traditions.

### 5.4 Historical and Community Knowledge

- Local histories.
- Historical documents.
- Community archives.
- Agricultural knowledge.
- Environmental knowledge.
- Civic and educational resources.

These areas are proposed research and preservation directions. They are not currently implemented capabilities of the software.

## 6. Community Participation

DukeAI Nexus aims to support meaningful participation by:

- Language speakers.
- Community historians.
- Educators.
- Linguists.
- Translators.
- Artists and cultural practitioners.
- Researchers.
- Cultural institutions.
- Open-source developers.

Participation should involve appropriate consultation, consent, attribution, and recognition.

Communities should have meaningful opportunities to influence how their knowledge is represented and used.

## 7. Cultural Rights and Consent

Cultural knowledge is not automatically unrestricted public data.

Some knowledge may be:

- Sacred.
- Restricted.
- Community-controlled.
- Privately held.
- Subject to traditional protocols.
- Unsuitable for public distribution.

DukeAI Nexus will not assume that all cultural material can be collected, digitised, redistributed, translated, or used for model training.

Appropriate permission and consultation must precede publication or reuse.

## 8. Technology Principles

Future language and cultural research should prioritise:

- Offline-first accessibility.
- Open-source tools.
- Resource-efficient processing.
- Source attribution.
- Transparent provenance.
- Human review.
- Language and dialect accuracy.
- Privacy and consent.
- Reproducible evaluation.
- Community participation.

## 9. Research Roadmap

Potential future phases include:

### Phase 1: Foundation

- Maintain the English and Kiswahili prototype.
- Improve local retrieval.
- Strengthen document governance.
- Establish evaluation procedures.

### Phase 2: Language Research

- Improve multilingual text processing.
- Identify potential language partners.
- Review available language resources.
- Develop community-reviewed evaluation material.

### Phase 3: Cultural Documentation

- Investigate appropriate documentation methods.
- Explore partnerships with cultural institutions.
- Develop attribution and consent procedures.
- Study ethical approaches to digital preservation.

### Phase 4: Expanded Local Technology

- Investigate translation systems.
- Benchmark compact language models.
- Explore local multilingual interfaces.
- Evaluate resource requirements on Android and Linux.

These phases are proposals and are dependent on implementation capacity, research results, partnerships, and permissions.

## 10. Risks and Responsibilities

Potential risks include:

- Misrepresentation of language varieties.
- Inaccurate translations.
- Unauthorised use of cultural material.
- Copyright infringement.
- Extraction of community knowledge without meaningful benefit.
- Overclaiming language coverage.
- Inadequate representation of regional differences.

Mitigation requires human review, transparent documentation, rights verification, community consultation, and clear reporting of limitations.

## 11. Expected Long-Term Contribution

The intended contribution is an open-source foundation that can support responsible African language technology and culturally respectful knowledge preservation.

Success should be measured not simply by the number of languages or documents collected, but by:

- Accuracy.
- Accessibility.
- Community participation.
- Respect for rights and permissions.
- Quality of documentation.
- Practical usefulness.
- Long-term sustainability.

## 12. Closing Vision

DukeAI Nexus seeks to contribute to an African AI research environment in which African languages, histories, creativity, and knowledge systems are represented with respect and technical integrity.

The ambition is to build useful technology while recognising that language and cultural preservation belong first and foremost to the people and communities who sustain them.

**Our Languages. Our Heritage. Our Future.**
